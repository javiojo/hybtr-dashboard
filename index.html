The Strategy

Most dividend trackers rank by yield alone — the bigger the number, the better the rank. This tracker does the opposite. Total return is the primary metric, because a 60% yield means nothing if the fund loses 50% of its value.

The HYBTR strategy focuses on dividend ETFs that deliver both: genuinely high income and positive capital growth. Funds that sacrifice share price to pay dividends are flagged as yield traps and surfaced as warnings, not recommendations.

Every ETF in the leaderboard is sorted descending by 1Y Total Return by default. ETFs without a 1Y Total Return on record show "—" for that column and sort to the bottom.

What Is a Yield Trap?

A yield trap is a fund that looks attractive on paper because of its high distribution yield, but is actively destroying capital. The rule applied here:

⚠  1Y Total Return < 0%

When 1Y Total Return is negative, the ETF is flagged in the leaderboard and counted in the yield trap indicator. No matter how high the yield, if you lost money overall, the distribution did not compensate — these are funds to avoid.

Why is the rule based on total return only? Because yield alone does not tell you whether you made or lost money. Every ETF in this tracker yields 3% or more — they are all dividend-paying by definition. What separates a good high-yield fund from a trap is whether it preserved or grew your capital. If the 1Y Total Return is negative, the fund paid out distributions while losing more value than it returned — that is a yield trap, regardless of how large the yield number looks.

What Is a Leveraged ETF? ⚠

Leveraged ETFs use derivatives and borrowed money to amplify the daily returns of an index — typically 2× or 3×. While they can generate outsized gains in a trending market, they also suffer from volatility decay: in choppy or sideways markets they lose value rapidly, even if the underlying index ends flat.

These funds are designed for short-term trading, not long-term holding. High headline yields on leveraged ETFs often reflect return-of-capital distributions rather than income, and their 1Y total return numbers can be extremely volatile. They appear in this tracker because they clear the screener criteria, but the ⚠ caution badge marks them as high-risk instruments not suitable for most income investors.
