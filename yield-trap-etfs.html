Yield Trap ETFs: When High Yield Hides Losses

Target keyword: yield trap ETFs. HYBTR is a research tool, not financial advice.

What is a yield trap?

A yield trap is a fund that looks attractive because of a high distribution yield, but is actively destroying capital. On HYBTR, every listed ETF yields 3% or more — they are all dividend-paying by definition. What separates a useful high-yield fund from a trap is whether it preserved or grew your capital after distributions.

HYBTR's rule: 1-year total return below 0%

The rule applied on the tracker is simple: if 1-year total return is less than 0%, the ETF is flagged as a yield trap and counted in the yield-trap indicator. No matter how high the yield, if the 1-year total return is negative, the distribution did not compensate for the loss in value.

Why yield alone is not enough

Most dividend trackers rank by yield alone. HYBTR ranks by total return instead, because a very large yield means little if the fund loses a similar share of its value. A negative 1-year total return means the fund paid distributions while losing more value than it returned.

How to screen yield trap ETFs in HYBTR

On the HYBTR dashboard, use the “Yield traps only” filter to isolate flagged funds, or turn on “Positive only” under 1Y Return to hide them. The interactive leaderboard marks each yield trap with a warning badge. Funds without a 1-year total return on record show “—” and sort to the bottom; they are not scored as traps until that figure exists.
